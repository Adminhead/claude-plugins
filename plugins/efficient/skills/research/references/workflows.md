# Research workflows

Use the smallest workflow that can support the decision. Depth is about consequence, uncertainty, and disagreement—not a preset time, source, or query count.

## Frame the work before retrieval

Write a compact research frame:

- **Decision question:** what must be decided, explained, or compared?
- **Audience and use:** who will act on the result and how?
- **Scope:** entities, geography, population, constraints, and explicit exclusions.
- **Time horizon:** historical, current, or future-looking; add an as-of date for changing facts.
- **Decision criteria:** the properties that would change the conclusion.
- **Assumptions and unknowns:** label assumptions; identify the smallest evidence that could overturn a material one.

Clarify only if missing information materially changes those fields. Otherwise proceed with a stated bounded assumption. Split the question into subquestions only where a separate answer makes source selection, comparison, or uncertainty clearer. Drop subquestions that cannot affect the decision.

## Select a depth

### Quick

Use for a narrow, low-consequence question that can be answered from a directly relevant, authoritative source.

1. Frame the question and as-of date if relevant.
2. Find the source that owns the claim; use a search result only to locate it.
3. Read the relevant content and record the claim, source, date/version, and passage or data.
4. Answer directly with a citation and any meaningful limitation.

Stop when the source directly supports the answer and no material ambiguity remains. Escalate to standard when the source is indirect, dated, inaccessible, or the question affects a material choice.

### Standard

Use for a comparison, recommendation, several material claims, or a question where one source may be incomplete.

1. Frame the decision and criteria.
2. Create a source plan: which claims need primary evidence, which need current status, and where independent corroboration would matter.
3. Retrieve candidates, read the source content, and maintain the ledger.
4. Check directness, date/version, applicability, and independence before treating sources as corroboration.
5. Compare evidence against the criteria; resolve or expose disagreement.
6. Audit each material claim and report a concise answer with traceable citations.

Stop when each decision-critical claim has proportionate support, uncertainty is explicit, and another source is unlikely to change the answer.

### Deep

Use for consequential, contentious, broad, or rapidly changing questions where evidence quality or scope can alter the decision.

1. Define the decision, stakes, scope, time horizon, criteria, material assumptions, and a research budget or stop trigger.
2. Map decision-critical subquestions and their expected evidence types. Prioritize uncertain or irreversible decision points.
3. Retrieve primary and authoritative material first; use secondary material to find sources, contextualize, or identify disputes.
4. Read sources directly. Record provenance, methods or definitions where relevant, dates, scope, and limitations in the ledger.
5. Deliberately test independence: separate original reporting or data from derivative coverage and avoid counting copies as confirmation.
6. Triangulate contested or high-impact claims; examine disagreement rather than averaging it away.
7. Synthesize facts, inferences, alternatives, and residual uncertainty against the decision criteria.
8. Perform a claim-level support audit and stop at the defined boundary unless new evidence can change the decision.

Deep research is not an obligation to produce a long report, spawn subagents, or collect a quota. Use parallel subagents only when several independent subquestions each need their own reading. If an access, time, or evidence boundary prevents a conclusion, package the strongest supported partial result and the next decision-relevant gap.

## Evidence ledger

Keep the ledger concise and proportional. A row, note, or table entry can contain:

| Claim or subquestion | Source and owner | Date/version | Relevant passage, data, or observation | Quality/applicability notes | Support status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | | direct / partial / conflicting / unsupported |

Record what the source actually says or measures before paraphrasing it. Distinguish a source’s conclusion from your interpretation. Note access barriers, unclear definitions, and relationships between sources that affect independence.

## Bounded evidence loop

For each decision-critical claim: identify the needed evidence, retrieve a source likely to provide it, read it, update the ledger, assess whether it changes the conclusion, and either stop or seek a specific missing discriminator. Do not keep searching because more material exists.

Useful stop triggers are:

- the scoped decision criteria are answered with proportionate support;
- remaining gaps or disagreements are explicit and do not block the stated decision;
- a predeclared time or access boundary is reached; or
- further retrieval cannot plausibly change the conclusion.

If an answer remains blocked, report the exact missing source, definition, access, or stakeholder decision rather than overstating confidence.
